Clarity about our data

Weather & forecasts

Open-Meteo ↗ supplies model-derived current conditions, hourly forecasts, and 7-day outlooks. Current conditions are estimates, not a local weather-station observation. Data is cached for 10 minutes.

Geography & radar

Map tiles and geography by OpenStreetMap contributors. Past precipitation radar by RainViewer. Radar coverage varies, especially in India. Blank radar does not mean no rain. Forecast radar is shown only if supplied.

Map layers

Temperature, clouds, wind, humidity, and pressure display sampled current Open-Meteo model values, not continuous radar or satellite imagery. Pan the map to fetch a new sample grid. A layer timestamp is the provider's model time.

Atmospheric monitoring

Open-Meteo Air Quality API ↗ supplies hourly model/forecast concentrations when available. SO₂, NO₂, PM2.5, PM10, and ozone are displayed using provider units and timestamps; they are not ground-station readings or rainwater pH measurements. The provider uses CAMS-backed grids (about 11 km in Europe and 45 km globally) and does not identify the contributing model in each response. Values are shown at the provider-returned grid centre, not as field measurements. Weather rainfall is separate forecast/model context. Read provider attribution and scientific limitations in docs/DATA_SOURCES.md.

Assistant & advisories

WeatherGPT uses a local NLP pipeline: Compromise for English tokens and place entities, plus weather-specific English/Hindi interpretation rules. It is not an LLM. Text analysis runs in your browser; extracted place names are sent to the weather/geocoding provider. Recommendations use the available forecast. Advisories are derived from stated thresholds, not official warnings. Browser speech recognition may send audio to your browser's speech provider.

Demonstration, not safety-critical software

Public APIs can be delayed, rate-limited, or unavailable. Verify official warnings before making safety-critical decisions. No satellite, lightning detection, or storm-track prediction is connected. Commercial use requires a review of provider licenses.

Villages and place search

Open-Meteo geocoding (GeoNames-derived, non-commercial use), then Photon and, for explicit searches only, OSM Nominatim ↗. Map and geocoding data © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL). Duplicates are shown as choices; the app never silently picks one.

Rainwater acidity, soil pH and irrigation water

These are three different things. No current, local, machine-readable measured rainwater pH dataset is connected, so the app shows “Rainwater acidity data unavailable for this location.” Research and sources are in docs/DATA_SOURCES.md. User-entered readings are labelled “User-provided” and stay on this device. The app never recommends soil treatment from a rainwater reading.

Crop precautions

Forecast hazards are calculated from the loaded forecast with configurable thresholds. Agricultural precautions are either sourced (ICAR / IARI / IMD agromet bulletins, cited in the knowledge base) or clearly labelled “general precaution, no crop-specific source found”. No pesticide or fertiliser doses are given. For product decisions contact your nearest KVK or state agriculture department. Weather alone cannot diagnose crop disease.

Spatial resolution

Open-Meteo documents model resolutions from about 1–2 km (regional) to 9–11 km (global); the API does not disclose which model produced a given value. Values are for a model grid cell, not your field. See Open-Meteo docs ↗.
